## Why this direction merits one experiment

**Primary-source observation:** RTA’s website-redesign Q&A tells vendors to include accessibility testing, certification, and audit services in their proposals. It also distributes migration, training, and other expectations across numerous answers. That provides a concrete example of requirements extending beyond a single RFP narrative. ([transitrta.com](https://www.transitrta.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/QA_RFP-020-25_Website-Redesign_Lot-2.pdf))

**Failure evidence:** In its February 5, 2026 *Morrish-Wallace* decision, GAO sustained a protest because the awardee had not acknowledged an amendment changing a construction specification. This is a federal construction case—not evidence about web agencies—but demonstrates that overlooked changes can have consequential outcomes. ([gao.gov](https://www.gao.gov/products/b-423796.2))

**Founder hypothesis:** A small agency without a dedicated proposal manager might benefit from a reviewed, source-linked checklist more than another proposal-writing tool.

The proposed deliverable is:

- One row per required submission item or performance obligation.
- Document, version, page, and section supporting each row.
- A separate list of changed requirements and unresolved ambiguities.
- Response-location and owner fields for the agency to complete.
- Explicit exclusions: no legal certification, bid submission, deadline-monitoring promise, or guarantee of completeness.

**Tradeoff:** This direction offers accessible test material and a narrowly scoped deliverable, but existing requirements-analysis software makes differentiation uncertain. The experiment should test whether the proposed review method is dependable before considering a business around it. ([help.responsive.io](https://help.responsive.io/en-US/responsive/article/Em6Zeijf-document-shredding-overview))

## One preregistered experiment

**Name:** RFP checklist reliability gate  
**Status:** Proposed; not executed. Zero measured outcomes.

**Smallest consequential assumption:** The proposed method can extract explicit requirements and reconcile changes without silently dropping important items or inventing obligations.

### Bounds

- **Window:** September 14–20, 2026.
- **Budget:** $0 incremental spending; eight founder-hours maximum.
- Allocate six hours to preparation and scoring; reserve two for error review and recordkeeping.
- Public documents only. No accounts, outreach, purchases, deployment, or customer commitments.

### Protocol

1. Select **three closed public-sector website RFP packages**, each with an original solicitation and at least one amendment or Q&A document. Freeze the document manifest before testing; use chronological selection among eligible packages rather than choosing easy examples.
2. Manually create reference checklists with page-level evidence before generating candidate outputs. Mark deadlines, required forms, submission methods, acknowledgments, and explicitly mandatory items as *critical*.
3. Freeze one extraction-and-review procedure. Run it on each package without supplying the reference checklist.
4. Score omissions, unsupported obligations, incorrect source references, and unresolved contradictions. Count missed requirements in amendments as omissions.
5. Record preparation and correction time separately. Founder-created reference labels are fallible; this is an internal benchmark, not independent validation.

### Fixed success criteria

All three packages must meet:

- **100% recall of reference-labelled critical requirements.**
- **At least 95% recall of all reference requirements.**
- **Zero unsupported obligations presented as mandatory.**
- **Correct source references for every accepted checklist row.**
- **No more than 30 minutes of correction work per package.**

These thresholds are proposed screening criteria, not measured performance.

**Stop condition:** Stop at eight hours or September 20, whichever comes first. Any critical omission or invented mandatory obligation fails the gate. If three complete packages cannot be obtained within budget, record *inconclusive*, not success. Do not tune the procedure and rescore the same fixtures as a fresh pass.

**Decision rule:** A pass justifies a separately preregistered, permissioned usability test when execution capabilities permit. It does not justify claiming time savings, customer demand, or launching a subscription. A failure supports revising the method or stopping.
